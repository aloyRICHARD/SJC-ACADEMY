Overview

Course overview

This course provides an in-depth analysis of Uganda's journey through Westernization, protest, and modernity. It explores the interplay of historical events, cultural shifts, and social movements that have influenced Uganda's development. Students will engage with diverse materials, including historical texts, literature, films, and visual arts, to understand the complexities of Ugandan society.

Course flow

How the 10 modules progress

The course moves from Uganda's foundations, through colonial impact and independence, into protest movements, modernity, the arts, literature and media, then gender, identity, and contemporary issues — closing with a capstone project.

Learning outcomes

Gain a comprehensive understanding of Uganda's history, culture, and contemporary issues.

Who it's for

The ideal student

Someone with a deep interest in African studies, particularly Uganda, and a background in social sciences, cultural studies, or related fields — strong analytical skills, cultural sensitivity, and a passion for exploring the intersections of history, politics, and culture.

Academic interests & goals

  • African studies — history, politics, culture, and social issues
  • History and sociology — historical processes and cultural transformations
  • Political science — political movements and Western influence on African states
  • Cultural studies — cultural expressions and societal representation

Relevant academic background

  • Social sciences — sociology, anthropology, history, political science, international relations
  • Cultural and media studies — visual arts, media, literature
  • Development studies — globalization and socio-economic issues in the Global South
  • Art and literature — film studies and visual arts

Personal attributes & skills

  • Analytical and critical thinking to analyze complex social phenomena
  • Cultural sensitivity, particularly within African contexts
  • Strong research skills across historical, literary, and visual media
  • Excellent written and verbal communication skills

Professional experience & aspirations

  • Academics and researchers pursuing African studies research
  • NGO and development workers in African development and social issues
  • Educators expanding African history and culture into their curricula
  • Journalists and media professionals producing informed content on African societies

Career aspirations

  • Academia and research in African studies, history, sociology, or cultural studies
  • Policy and advocacy careers focused on African affairs
  • Creative industries — artists, filmmakers, writers engaging African themes
  • Global development, focusing on socio-cultural aspects of development in Africa

Geographical & cultural interest

  • Strong interest in Uganda specifically, or Sub-Saharan Africa generally
  • Cultural enthusiasts passionate about exploring diverse cultures, histories, and social movements
